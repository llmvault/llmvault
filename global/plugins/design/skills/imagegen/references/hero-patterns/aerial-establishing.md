# Aerial / establishing hero

A drone or high-vantage wide shot conveying scale, scope, and ambition: a construction site from above, farmland to the horizon, a port at dawn, a city grid at dusk. For construction, real estate, logistics, agriculture, energy, and municipalities — industries whose product is physical scale.

## What the layout does to the image

- Almost always full-bleed with overlaid copy — so a quiet zone is required, from sky (oblique angle) or from a calm region of ground pattern (top-down).
- The graphic, pattern-like quality of aerial views tolerates text overlay better than ground-level photos — repetitive texture reads as background.
- Often darkened slightly by CSS; dawn/dusk scenes survive this best.

## Camera angle decision — the defining choice

- **Oblique (30–45°, "high drone view looking across")**: shows depth, horizon, and sky — natural quiet zone in the sky area, more cinematic. Default for heroes. The model drifts toward *steeper* angles than asked; anchor with horizon language: "the horizon line sits in the upper quarter of the frame".
- **Top-down (90°, "directly overhead, satellite-like")**: pure graphic geometry, near-abstract, striking — but no sky, so the quiet zone must be a calm ground region (water, empty field, fresh concrete). Better for editorial/section images than headline-bearing heroes.

## Image requirements

- Strong geometry: grids, diagonals, repeated units (containers, crop rows, foundations) — the aerial's beauty is pattern.
- One accent element cutting the pattern: a single yellow crane's diagonal, one red truck on an empty road, a boat's wake.
- Scale cues: tiny workers, vehicles, long shadows (specify "late afternoon" or "early morning" for shadow drama).
- Named quiet zone per the angle chosen (sky region or calm ground region).

## Aspect ratio

`16:9`.

## Example prompts

Construction development (oblique, text-ready sky):

> Aerial drone photograph from a high oblique angle looking across a large construction site at early morning, the horizon line sitting in the upper quarter of the frame. Below, the geometric grid of foundations and access roads spreads across the frame, orange excavators and tiny workers casting long shadows, one yellow tower crane cutting a clean diagonal through the pattern. The upper quarter is soft hazy dawn sky in pale gold, calm and empty. Crisp detail, premium documentary grade. No text, no letters, no logos.

Port / logistics (top-down, graphic):

> Aerial photograph taken directly overhead, satellite-like, of a container terminal at golden hour. Stacked shipping containers form crisp rectangular color blocks — rust red, navy, teal — across the right two thirds of the frame, cut by one long empty quay of smooth pale concrete along the left third where a single tiny red straddle carrier travels. Long dramatic shadows from the low sun, near-abstract geometry, high crisp detail. No text, no letters, no logos.

## Pitfalls

- Ask for 45° and you may get 90° — the horizon-position clause ("horizon in the upper quarter") is the reliable anchor for oblique shots.
- Top-down with no calm region gives text nowhere to live; always name the empty quay/field/water and its position.
- Uniform texture with no accent element reads as wallpaper; the single contrasting object is what makes it a hero.
