# Textured editorial hero

Hand-crafted, art-directed illustration styles with visible physical texture: risograph prints, paper-cut collage, painterly gouache/oil. For brands that want warmth, craft, and personality — studios, publications, food, culture, boutique products. The strongest antidote to generic AI-image look.

## What the layout does to the image

- Used full-width behind copy (if the artwork has a built-in quiet zone) or in a split column.
- The texture IS the brand statement — it must survive compression and scaling, so favor bold shapes with texture, not texture alone.
- These styles carry strong internal color identities; the page's palette should follow the artwork, not fight it.

## Style menu — all execute with high fidelity

- **Risograph**: "textured risograph print style, bold flat shapes, visible grain, slight ink misregistration, two-color print in [color] and [color] with cream paper showing through." Punchy, indie, editorial.
- **Paper-cut collage**: "layered paper-cut collage, overlapping sheets of colored card, each layer casting a soft real drop shadow, visible paper texture and cut edges, like a shadowbox diorama photographed straight on." Tactile, dimensional; layers naturally fill frames, and a dark foreground band doubles as a text zone.
- **Painterly**: "painted with loose confident brushstrokes, thick visible impasto paint texture" + an era anchor ("evokes WPA-era industrial posters", "mid-century travel poster"). Atmospheric, premium, emotional.
- **Line art + spot color**: "confident thin contour line drawing on warm cream, dense linework reaching every edge, with only two or three elements filled in flat spot color." Elegant, architectural. Highest risk of letter-like artifacts — strengthen the no-text clause.

## Image requirements

- Bold silhouettes and a dynamic composition (diagonals, layered depth) — texture on a static composition reads as a filter, not a style.
- A named, limited palette (2–4 colors for riso/collage, warm-family for painterly).
- If text will overlay: build the quiet zone INTO the artwork ("a solid dark paper band spans the bottom third", "the sky area is a single flat cream field").

## Aspect ratio

`16:9` full-width; `4:3`/`1:1` for columns.

## Example prompts

Risograph (construction/trades brand):

> Editorial illustration in a textured risograph print style, the artwork covering the entire frame with no borders. A construction crew raising a steel beam at dawn, rendered in bold flat shapes with visible grain and slight ink misregistration, like a two-color riso print in navy blue and warm orange with cream paper showing through. Dynamic diagonal composition: the beam cuts across the frame, silhouetted workers guide it, a crane anchors the right edge, textured sun rays fill the sky. Hand-crafted, warm, magazine-cover quality. No text, no letters, no logos.

Paper collage (community/culture brand):

> Layered paper-cut collage illustration covering the entire frame edge to edge: a lively neighborhood street built from overlapping sheets of colored card — navy, mustard, burnt orange, dusty pink and cream — each layer casting a soft real drop shadow. Foreground: a solid deep-navy paper band spanning the bottom quarter with silhouetted paper figures walking along it. Middle layers: shopfronts and trees cut from card; background: paper clouds and a big paper sun reaching the top corners. Visible paper texture, shadowbox depth, photographed straight on. No text, no letters, no logos.

## Pitfalls

- These styles are distinctive — confirm they fit the brand before generating; a riso hero on a corporate law firm reads as a mistake.
- Painterly prompts drift toward "oil painting" regardless of the paint named; anchor with an era/poster reference for control.
- Line art needs "no text, no letters, no writing, no numbers" — stray glyph-like marks are its signature failure.
