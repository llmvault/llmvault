# Writing Slides

Write slide copy for fast comprehension and persuasion. A slide is not a document page; it is a designed decision surface.

## Headline Patterns

**Statement:** a claim that takes a position.

- "Manual QA is blocking release velocity"
- "Security review now starts before procurement"
- "The market is ready for workflow-native AI"

**Question:** create tension or invite reflection.

- "What breaks if onboarding doubles next quarter?"
- "Which risk are we actually trying to remove?"

**Action:** make the desired move clear.

- "Approve the pilot for the enterprise segment"
- "Move pricing validation before launch"

**Framing:** orient the section without being vague.

- "What we learned from the first 50 customers"
- "Where the business case is strongest"

Headlines should usually be 4-14 words, sentence case, and no trailing period. For async decks, make them complete enough to survive forwarding.

## Body Text Rules

- Use **bold lead-in + explanation** when bullets need structure: `**Retention is the constraint.** New signups are healthy, but month-two activation is weak.`
- Use 2-4 bullets, not a dense list.
- Put numbers close to the claim they prove.
- Replace abstract adjectives with evidence. "Fast" is weaker than "Cuts review time from 4 days to 6 hours."
- Use audience language from discovery, customer quotes, or the user's brief.
- Cut anything the presenter will say aloud unless the deck is async.

## Slide Templates

### statement

- **Headline:** bold claim or insight.
- **Supporting:** optional one to two lines, metric, or screenshot callout.

### big-statement

- **Headline only:** full-slide claim.
- **Use when:** transition, dramatic reframing, or memorable takeaway.

### question

- **Headline:** provocative question.
- **Supporting:** optional one-line context.

### data

- **Headline:** what the data shows, not "Data".
- **Metrics:** two to four key numbers with labels and source.
- **Annotation:** call out the change that matters.

### product-proof

- **Headline:** outcome or workflow change.
- **Visual:** screenshot, before/after, or flow.
- **Callouts:** two to three annotations max.

### goals

- **Headline:** outcome framing, not just "Agenda".
- **Points:** three bullets tied to decisions or takeaways.

### recap

- **Headline:** "What this means" or a sharper synthesis.
- **Points:** one complete thought per core section.

### next-steps

- **Headline:** direct ask.
- **Points:** owner, date, action, dependency, or CTA.

## Conversion Copy Rules

- Put the audience's desired outcome before your product or team.
- Name the cost of staying still.
- Pair every claim with proof or a clear mechanism.
- Treat objections as slides, not footnotes.
- End with one primary action.

## Transformation Examples

**Before:** "Overview of Q3 performance metrics and results"

**After:** "Q3 expansion revenue grew despite slower pipeline creation"

**Before:** "Our product strategy is based on reusable components"

**After:** "Reusable building blocks let teams launch workflows faster"

**Before:** "Next steps"

**After:** "Approve the 30-day pilot so onboarding can start Monday"

## Writing Workflow

1. Identify the one thing the audience must remember from this slide.
2. Write the headline first as a claim, question, or action.
3. Add body text only if it helps the audience understand, believe, or decide.
4. Check the slide at presentation size. If it cannot be parsed quickly, simplify.
5. In Canvas, rewrite or resize copy when it breaks the layout.
