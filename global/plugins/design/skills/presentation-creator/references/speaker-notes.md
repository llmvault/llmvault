# Speaker Notes

Speaker notes are scannable prompts for natural delivery, not scripts to read verbatim.

## Per-Slide Note Structure

```markdown
### Slide [N]: [Headline]

**Key point:** [The one thing they must remember]

**Open with:** [First sentence or hook in a conversational tone]

**Talk track:**
- [Prompt 1]
- [Prompt 2]
- [Optional anecdote, example, or proof]

**Audience check:** [Question to ask, if useful]

**Transition:** [Bridge to next slide]
```

## Notes by Slide Type

- **Statement/question:** explain what led to the conclusion and why it matters.
- **Data:** state what the number means, why it changed, and what decision it supports.
- **Product proof:** narrate the user scenario, not every UI detail.
- **Objection:** acknowledge the concern directly, then show proof or mitigation.
- **Section divider:** keep it short; connect what came before to what comes next.
- **Recap:** synthesize rather than repeat every slide.

## Delivery Cues

Use only when they help:

- `(pause)` to let a point land.
- `(ask)` for audience interaction.
- `(click)` for purposeful reveal or build.
- `(emphasize)` for vocal stress.
- `(scan room)` before a decision or ask.

## Context Adjustments

- **Internal:** candid, concise, direct about tradeoffs.
- **External:** prove before concluding, define terms, leave room for questions.
- **Executive:** lead with recommendation and business implication.
- **Sales/demo:** keep checking relevance to their situation.
- **Recorded/async:** use notes sparingly; add essential context to the slide itself.

## What to Avoid

- Full scripts that invite reading aloud.
- Repeating the exact slide text.
- Long stories with no transition.
- Notes that introduce claims not supported by the slide.
