# Business and Conversion Decks

Use this reference for sales decks, proposal decks, demo decks, executive briefings, board updates, launch decks, and business presentations where the audience should take action.

## First Principles

- The audience is the hero. Their problem, risk, opportunity, and desired future drive the deck.
- One meeting has one primary objective. Do not try to educate, sell, onboard, and negotiate in the same deck.
- Conversion depends on confidence. Confidence comes from relevance, proof, risk reduction, and a clear next step.
- A deck should create a decision, not just transmit information.

## Required Inputs

Collect or infer:

- Audience role: economic buyer, technical buyer, user, investor, board, internal stakeholder, or mixed.
- Stage: discovery, demo, proposal, approval, renewal, funding, launch, or alignment.
- Desired action: book, approve, buy, fund, adopt, decide, continue, or share.
- Current pain: what is not working now.
- Cost of status quo: money, time, risk, churn, delay, missed revenue, or morale.
- Proof available: metrics, case studies, screenshots, testimonials, security, logos, product evidence.
- Objection: budget, timing, trust, implementation, risk, priority, switching cost, or competition.

## Deck Structures

### Executive Briefing

```text
Purpose -> Recommendation -> Why now -> Evidence -> Risk -> Investment -> Decision
```

- Lead with the recommendation.
- Keep detail layered. Use backup slides for deep dives.
- Tie every slide to business impact, risk, or decision quality.

### Sales or Proposal Deck

```text
What we heard -> Cost of the problem -> Recommended path -> Proof -> Implementation -> Investment -> Next step
```

- Use the prospect's words.
- Show that the recommendation is specific to them.
- Present price as investment with expected return, risk removal, or business value.

### Demo Deck

```text
Your goal -> Workflow today -> What changes -> Product proof -> Customer proof -> Next step
```

- Do not show every feature.
- Show the top use cases from discovery.
- Pause for reaction after each major product moment.

### Board or Internal Business Deck

```text
Headline result -> What changed -> Drivers -> Risks -> Decisions needed -> Next actions
```

- Start with the punchline.
- Separate facts, interpretation, and recommendation.
- Highlight decisions explicitly.

## Slide Jobs

Each slide should do one of these jobs:

- **Context:** frame what the audience needs to understand.
- **Tension:** make the cost or risk of inaction visible.
- **Proposal:** show the recommended path.
- **Proof:** make the claim believable.
- **Risk removal:** answer a likely objection.
- **Action:** make the next step clear.

If a slide does none of these, cut it.

## Proof Patterns

- **Metric:** one number with a clear label, source, and implication.
- **Before/after:** current workflow compared to target workflow.
- **Case study:** customer, challenge, solution, result.
- **Screenshot:** product moment with two to three callouts.
- **Quote:** real language that reveals pain or confidence.
- **Comparison:** alternatives and why the recommendation wins.
- **Roadmap:** what happens after approval.

## Objection Handling

Convert common objections into designed slides:

- **Budget:** show ROI, payback, avoided cost, or phased scope.
- **Timing:** show urgency, rollout path, and low-friction start.
- **Trust:** show customer proof, security, compliance, or implementation record.
- **Complexity:** show the first milestone and who owns each step.
- **Competition:** compare by buyer criteria, not vendor boasting.

## CTA and Ask

The final slide should be concrete:

```text
Approve [specific action] by [date] so [outcome] can happen.
```

Include:

- Action.
- Owner.
- Date.
- What happens next.
- What the audience gets if they say yes.

Weak ask: "Questions?"

Strong ask: "Approve the 30-day pilot by Friday so onboarding can start Monday."

## Canvas Notes

For business decks, make proof visually scannable:

- Put metrics in large type with small source labels.
- Use screenshots and diagrams when they shorten explanation.
- Keep every proof slide tied to the claim in the headline.
- Reserve appendix slides for extra data, objections, security, implementation, or financial detail.
